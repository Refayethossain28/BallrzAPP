How it reaches the internet: a static page can't read cross-origin pages by itself — that's the browser's same-origin policy doing its job. So without a server, Magpie tries the page directly and then falls back to a public CORS relay (allorigins → corsproxy → codetabs, first one that answers) — zero setup, whole internet, but be aware the relay is a third party that sees which URLs you scrape and can be slow or down. robots.txt is respected either way — the rules are fetched per-origin and evaluated by the same unit-tested parser. For private, reliable fetching run the zero-dependency companion server (node magpie/server.mjs): it identifies itself as MagpieBot, refuses private/internal addresses, and fetches one page at a time with a polite delay. Scrape responsibly: public pages, sensible volumes, and honour each site's terms. Everything you extract stays on your device.
