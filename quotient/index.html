    How Quotient scores you

    Every item is generated

    Matrix grids, number series, rotation figures, digit spans and logic puzzles are built from a random seed the moment you need them, with their difficulty set by their structure: how many rules a matrix combines, the order of a series rule, the length of a span, the form of an argument. No two sittings share items, so nothing can be looked up or memorised. Only the verbal items come from a fixed bank, and a sitting never repeats one.

    The test adapts

    Every item carries item-response-theory parameters — how sharply it separates abilities, how hard it is, and how likely a guess is to land. After each answer your ability is re-estimated (an expected-a-posteriori estimate under a wide normal prior), and the next item is drawn at the difficulty that will tell us most about you. Domains are rotated so the profile stays balanced.

    The score and its interval

    Ability is expressed in standard-deviation units and mapped to the familiar scale: mean 100, SD 15. The 95% interval is the posterior uncertainty of that estimate. A standard sitting usually lands around ±11 points — that is the honest resolution of a 25-minute test, and any test that quotes tighter without a longer sitting is overstating itself.

    What it is not

    Item difficulties are model-based, not calibrated on a norming sample, so the scale is an informed approximation of population IQ, not a clinical measurement. Fatigue, language, distraction and practice all move scores by several points. If the number matters for a real decision, take a professionally administered test.
